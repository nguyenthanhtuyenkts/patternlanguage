### Problem
>If you spend eight hours of your day at work, and eight hours at home, there is no reason why your workplace should be any less of a community than your home.

### Solution
>Build or encourage the formation of work communities—each one a collection of smaller clusters of workplaces which have their own courtyards, gathered round a larger common square or common courtyard which contains shops and lunch counters. The total work community should have no more than 10 or 20 workplaces in it.

### Related Patterns
... according to the pattern [[9. Scattered Work]], work is entirely decentralized and woven in and out of the housing areas. The effect of [[9. Scattered Work]] can be increased piecemeal, by building individual work communities, one by one, in the boundaries between the neighborhoods; these work communities will then help to form the boundaries -- [[13. Subculture Boundary]], [[15. Neighborhood Boundary]] -- and above all in the boundaries, they will help to form [[30. Activity Nodes]].

Make the square at the heart of the community a public square with public paths coming through it [[61. Small Public Squares]]; either in this square, or in some attached space, place opportunities for sports -- [[72. Local Sports]]; make sure that the entire community is always within three minutes' walk of an [[60. Accessible Green]]; lay out the individual smaller courtyards in such a way that people naturally gather there -- [[115. Courtyards Which Live]]; keep the workshops small -- [[80. Self-Governing Workshops and Offices]]; encourage communal cooking and eating over and beyond the lunch counters -- [[88. Street Cafe]], [[93. Food Stands]], [[147. Communal Eating]] ...
