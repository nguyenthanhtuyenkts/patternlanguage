### Problem
>Without common land no social system can survive.

### Solution
>Give over 25 percent of the land in house clusters to common land which touches, or is very near, the homes which share it. Basic: be wary of the automobile; on no account let it dominate this land.

### Related Patterns
... just as there is a need for public land at the neighborhood level - [[60. Accessible Green]], so also, within the clusters and work communities from which the neighborhoods are made, there is a need for smaller and more private kinds of common land shared by a few work groups or a few families. This common land, in fact, forms the very heart and soul of any cluster. Once it is defined, the individual buildings of the cluster form around it - [[37. House Cluster]], [[38. Row Houses]], [[39. Housing Hill]], [[41. Work Community]].

Shape the common land so it has some enclosure and good sunlight - [[105. South Facing Outdoors]], [[106. Positive Outdoor Space]]; and so that smaller and more private pieces of land and pockets always open onto it - [[114. Hierarchy of Open Space]]; provide communal functions within the land - [[69. Public Outdoor Room]], [[72. Local Sports]], [[177. Vegetable Garden]]; and connect the different and adjacent pieces of common land to one another to form swaths of connected play space - [[68. Connected Play]]. Roads can be part of common land if they are treated as [[51. Green Streets]] ...
