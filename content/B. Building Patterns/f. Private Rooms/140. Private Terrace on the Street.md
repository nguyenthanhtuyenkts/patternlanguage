### Problem
>The relationship of a house to a street is often confused: either the house opens entirely to the street and there is no privacy; or the house turns its back on the street, and communion with street life is lost.

### Solution
>Let the common rooms open onto a wide terrace of a porch which looks into the street. Raise the terrace slightly above street level and protect it with a low wall, which you can see over if you sit near it, but which prevents people on the street from looking into the common rooms.

### Related Patterns
... among the common areas and sitting spaces - [[129. Common Areas at the Heart]], [[142. Sequence of Sitting Spaces]] - there is a need for one, at least, which puts the people in the house in touch with the world of the street outside the house. This pattern helps to create the [[111. Half-Hidden Garden]] and gives life to the street - [[51. Green Streets]] or [[100. Pedestrian Street]].

If possible, place the terrace in a position which is also congruent with natural contours - [[169. Terraced Slope]]. The wall, if low enough, can be a [[243. Sitting Wall]]; in other cases, where you want more privacy, you can build a full garden wall, with openings in it, almost like windows, which make the connection with the street - [[173. Garden Wall]], [[193. Half-Open Wall]]. In any case, surround the terrace with enough things to give it at least the partial feeling of a room - [[163. Outdoor Room]] ...
