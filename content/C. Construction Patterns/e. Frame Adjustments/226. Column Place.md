### Problem
>Thin columns, spindly columns, columns which take their shape from structural arguments alone, will never make a comfortable environment.

### Solution
>When a column is free standing, make it as thick as a person—at least 12 inches, preferably 16 inches: and form places around it where people can sit and lean comfortably: a step, a small seat built up against the column, or a space formed by a pair of columns.

### Related Patterns
... certain columns, especially those which are free standing, play an important social role, beyond their structural role as [[212. Columns at the Corners]]. These are, especially, the columns which help to form arcades, galleries, porches, walkways, and outdoor rooms - [[69. Public Outdoor Room]], [[119. Arcades]], [[163. Outdoor Room]], [[166. Gallery Surround]], [[167. Six-Foot Balcony]], [[174. Trellised Walk]]. This pattern defines the character these columns need to make them function socially.

You can get the extra thickness quite cheaply if you build the column as a [[216. Box Columns]]; complete the "place" the column forms, by giving it a "roof" in the form of a column capital, or vault which springs from the column, or by bracing the column against the beams - [[227. Column Connections]]. And when it makes sense, make the column base a [[243. Sitting Wall]], a place for flowers - [[245. Raised Flowers]], or a place for a chair or table - [[251. Different Chairs]] ...
